# Chapter 1 coverage review: how much of what students are asked do the games teach?

Scope: **Class 12 Physics, Chapter 1 (Electric Charges and Fields) only.** Reviewed 2026-10-27 against the chapter, its 12 worked examples, its "Points to Ponder" and its exercises 1.1 to 1.23 (NCERT 2026-27 reprint, read locally), the seven built games, and the general shape of board and entrance questions. This file lists topics and exercise numbers only; it never reproduces NCERT text or figures.

How the exam evidence was gathered, and its limits:
- NCERT exercises and worked examples: read directly. These are reliable.
- Board-paper structure: a web check found the CBSE Class 12 paper has 1-mark MCQ/assertion-reason, 2-mark, 3-mark, 4-mark case-study and 5-mark long-answer parts, and that Electrostatics (Chapters 1 and 2 together) is worth roughly 16 of 70 theory marks (one blueprint posted by a user says 8, which looks wrong). **No source gives a Chapter 1 only mark split**, so the weights below are my judgement from the syllabus wording and typical papers, not measured. Treat them as a priority guide, not a fact. Check the official CBSE sample paper before relying on them.

> **Progress note (2026-10-27, after wave 1):** `net-force-workshop` now covers B2, B4, B6 and B7 and part of B1 and B3, so the tables below are the starting position. The current weighted coverage is in `ch01-concept-weights.md`.

## 1. Headline

| | Concept rows in the map | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Covered well (✅) | 3 (A1, A2, A4) | 8% |
| Covered partly (🟡) | 4 (A3, A5, A6, B1) | 11% |
| Not covered (⬜) | 29 | 81% |

- Games exist for sections **1.2 to 1.5 only** (charge, conductors, additivity, conservation, quantisation, Coulomb's law). Sections **1.6 to 1.14 have no game**: superposition, field, field lines, flux, dipole, torque, continuous charge, Gauss's law and its three applications.
- Those uncovered sections hold the questions that are asked most often and carry the most marks (dipole fields and torque, Gauss's law derivations and applications, superposition with vectors, flux, field lines). **The games currently cover the easiest part of the chapter, and the exam weight sits in the part with no games.**
- Of the 23 exercises, the games help with about 6 (1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.5, 1.11 and part of 1.12); the other 17 need new games.

## 6. Review of the seven built games for correctness and depth

| Game | Covers | Strengths | Gaps and risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curator's Spheres (A1) | Two kinds of charge; like repels, unlike attracts; neutral bodies are attracted by either sign; rubbing convention | Handles the "neutral can't be affected" misconception correctly; evidence gate stops guessing | Does not name polarisation as the reason a neutral body is attracted |
| Electron Ledger (A2) | Electrons move; both bodies charged with opposite signs; q = Ne; which material gives up electrons; level 1 reasons about attract and repel | Strong mix of reasoning, calculation and deduction | Mass transfer negligible (Ex 1.11b) missing. The material order is a common triboelectric series and differs between books: only glass/silk and similar pairs are guaranteed to match the student's textbook |
| Fuel Station Inspector (A3) | Conductors, insulators, discharge to earth | Series and parallel paths; cost and resistance trade-off | Does not teach that charge spreads over a conductor but stays put on an insulator, nor charging an insulated conductor (the "metal spoon" misconception). Resistance and cost go beyond this chapter's scope: fine as enrichment, but not exam content |
| Coulomb's Cabinet II (A4) | Algebraic sum of charges, prefixes | Exact arithmetic, sealed-capsule deduction | Very good on A4; scalar nature only implied |
| Share the Charge (A5) | Sharing between identical conductors; conservation in an isolated system | BFS-optimal par | Conservation only appears as "total stays the same". No "spot the process that breaks conservation" (nuclear or pair-production equations), and no Coulomb force after sharing (B4) |
| Millikan's Oil-Drop Lab (A6) | q = ne, finding e from samples, impure data | Real experimental reasoning | "Why quantisation can be ignored at everyday scale" and "number of electrons in 1 C" scale intuition are missing |
| Coulomb's Levitator (B1) | Coulomb force balancing weight; inverse square; unit handling; sign rule | Good application and prefix practice | It solves for charge. Direct force from F = kq₁q₂/r², solving for distance, scaling, and the vector form are not practised; k and ε₀ appear but ε₀ is never used |

## 8. Recommended build order (superseded: see the textbook-order waves in ch01-concept-weights.md)

Each should be a real game, not a quiz (see AGENTS.md section 5). Mechanic ideas are suggestions to agree with the owner first.

1. **Superposition and equilibrium** (B2, B3, B6, B7, C3): *"Charge Tuner"*: place or choose charges so a probe feels no net force, or a target net force. Covers Ex 1.6, 1.8 and the "zero-field point" questions. Needs a small vector sketch inside the game.
2. **Field lines** (D1, D2, C2): *"Field Detective"*: repair or choose the physically possible field-line picture; read charges from the picture. Covers Ex 1.7 and many 1-3 mark questions.
3. **Dipole** (F1 to F5): *"Compass of Charge"*: dipole in a field, set the angle for a torque, find the stable orientation, compare axis and equator fields. Covers Ex 1.9, 1.10 and the derivation questions.
4. **Flux** (E1, E2, H1b): *"Flux Gate"*: rotate a loop, a cube in a uniform field, check that net flux through a closed surface is zero.
5. **Gauss's law** (H1 to H7, G1): *"Gauss Lab"*: pick the Gaussian surface for a distribution, then probe inside and outside a shell, a line and a sheet. Covers Ex 1.17 to 1.23 and the three derivations.
6. **Charged particle in a field** (C4, C6): *"Particle Tracks"*: aim a particle and read tracks. Covers Ex 1.13.
7. **Fill the gaps in the built games**: direct F = kq₁q₂/r² and scaling (B1, B2, B4, B5 as a Coulomb "cabinet"); mass transfer and macro-scale quantisation as extra levels in Electron Ledger and the Oil-Drop Lab; charge spreading on conductors in the Fuel Station Inspector.
8. **A mixed "exam mode"**: a timed set of short mechanics that mixes assertion-reason style checks, once the above exist.

Cross-cutting skills still untaught: 2-D vector addition and symmetry cancellation, drawing and reading field diagrams, estimating before calculating.

## 9. How to keep this honest

- When a game is added, move its rows in `ch01-electric-charges-and-fields.md` and the tables above from ⬜ to 🟡 or ✅, and say *which exercise or question type it now covers*.
- A row is ✅ only when a student could answer the exam-style question after playing, not merely when the topic is mentioned.
- Re-run this review after every wave.
